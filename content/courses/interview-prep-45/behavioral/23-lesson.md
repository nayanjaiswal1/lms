## Why interviewers ask this

This checks whether you can grow other people, not just yourself — a signal companies weight heavily for senior and staff-track roles, and increasingly for anyone who'll eventually onboard a junior teammate. It also reveals whether you can adapt your explanation style to someone else's level, not just your own.

## The framework: STAR, weighted on their growth, not your effort

- **Situation** — who you mentored and what gap they had.
- **Task** — what you specifically took responsibility for teaching or unblocking.
- **Action** — your actual method: pairing, code review comments, a specific technique for letting them find the answer rather than just giving it to them.
- **Result** — evidence of their growth, specifically something they can now do independently that they couldn't before, not just "they said thanks."

The common weak version of this story is "I helped a junior engineer with their code" with no specifics about what changed in them. The strong version names a before/after in their capability.

## Worked example

> **Situation**: "A new grad on our team was technically capable but afraid to open PRs for anything beyond trivial changes — she'd sit on finished work for days second-guessing it. **Task**: I wasn't her manager, but I noticed the pattern in standup and decided to pair with her directly. **Action**: I started reviewing her draft PRs before she opened them publicly, but instead of fixing things myself, I'd leave comments like 'what happens if this list is empty?' and let her find the fix. After a few weeks I stopped offering to pre-review and told her I trusted her to open PRs directly — she pushed back, so I pointed to the last five PRs, where my comments had been minor at most. **Result**: Her PR turnaround time from 'code complete' to 'opened' dropped from days to hours within a month, and six months later she was doing the same pre-review-by-questions technique for the next new hire on the team, which is when I knew it had actually stuck rather than just made her dependent on me."

The story ends with her teaching someone else — that's the real proof of growth, not gratitude.

## Your template

```
Situation: [who you mentored, and the specific gap]
Task: [what you took responsibility for — even informally]
Action: [your method — questions over answers, pairing, staged independence]
Result: [concrete change in their capability] + [evidence it stuck without you]
```

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Name a specific before/after in their skill | Say "I helped them a lot" with no specifics |
| Show a method that builds independence, not dependence | Describe just doing the work for them |
| End with evidence the growth stuck after you stepped back | End at "they were grateful" |
