## Why this review matters

Week 2 added five more STAR stories — negotiation, innovation, overcoming an obstacle, your biggest success, learning something new fast — on top of the resume walkthrough and situational-question answers from Week 1. The risk at this checkpoint is momentum: it's easy to have written five stories without ever saying any of them out loud, and a story that only exists on paper isn't ready.

## The technique: verify before you build more

Don't treat this as a checkbox exercise. For each story you've written this week, say it out loud once, timed. If you stumble finding the point of the story, or it runs past three minutes, it isn't done — it's still a draft. Flag it now, not the night before an interview.

Then look forward. Week 3 shifts your daily time budget toward harder technical prep — DP hard problems, more system design reps, deeper backend fundamentals. That's the right move, but only if the behavioral side is solid enough that you're not scrambling to build stories the week before onsites.

## Doing the review

Score each of your Week 1 and Week 2 stories against the same three checks from Day 7: timing under three minutes, at least one concrete number or detail, and "I" language for your actions. Anything that still fails two of three gets flagged for a rewrite — Week 3's technical grind won't leave you spare time to fix it later.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Say each story out loud, timed, at least once | Assume a written draft is interview-ready |
| Flag weak stories now, before Week 3 starts | Discover a weak story mid-interview |
| Set a concrete Week 3 technical plan | Let "deepen backend knowledge" stay vague |
