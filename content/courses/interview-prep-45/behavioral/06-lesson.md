## Why interviewers ask this

Most candidates for individual-contributor roles don't have "manager" on their resume, so "tell me about a time you showed leadership" is really asking about **leadership without authority**: did you see a problem nobody owned and move on it anyway, or did you wait to be told. Companies promote and level people based on exactly this signal.

## The framework: STAR, weighted on initiative

Same STAR shape as Day 4-5. The distinguishing element for a leadership story is the **Task** section: you need to make clear that nobody assigned this to you. If your manager told you to do it, it's a project story, not a leadership story.

- **Situation**: the gap or problem nobody was addressing.
- **Task**: that you chose to take this on, and why you were positioned to.
- **Action**: how you got buy-in from people who didn't report to you. This is the crux: leadership without authority is persuasion, not command.
- **Result**: the outcome, plus evidence others followed your lead (adoption, a teammate crediting you, the practice sticking after you moved on).

## Worked example

> **Situation**: "Our team had no consistent code review standard. Reviews ranged from a thumbs-up in 30 seconds to week-long back-and-forths with no clear bar, and new hires had no idea what 'good' looked like. **Task**: Nobody asked me to fix this, but I was getting frustrated re-explaining the same review comments to different people, so I decided to write something down. **Action**: I drafted a one-page review checklist based on the comments I saw repeated most, things like 'error messages include context' and 'no magic numbers without a named constant,' and instead of mandating it, I posted it in our team channel and asked for feedback for a week. I incorporated two pushbacks that were genuinely good points, then proposed we link it in our PR template as a soft guideline, not a gate. **Result**: Within a month, review turnaround time dropped because reviewers had a shared bar instead of relitigating style each time, and two other teams in the org asked to adapt it for themselves. I didn't have to chase adoption. The fact that it solved a real pain point did that for me."

The persuasion mechanism, a public draft with incorporated pushback and no mandate, is the actual leadership skill being demonstrated.

## Your template

```
Situation: [the gap or problem nobody owned]
Task: [why you took it on: nobody assigned it to you]
Action: [how you got others to adopt or follow: persuasion, not authority]
Result: [outcome] + [evidence others followed: adoption, spread, credit]
```

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Pick something nobody assigned you | Reuse a story where your manager told you to do it |
| Show how you got buy-in without authority | Describe just announcing a decision and everyone complying |
| Give evidence the change stuck | End at "I proposed it" with no follow-through |
| Keep the story scoped, one initiative, not your whole career | Try to cram three examples into one answer |
