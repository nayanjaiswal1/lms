## Why interviewers ask this

Negotiation questions check whether you can push for what a project needs — more time, less scope, a different resourcing plan — without either caving immediately or turning it into a fight. It's a proxy for how you'll behave when a PM hands you an unrealistic deadline, which happens on every team eventually.

## The framework: STAR, weighted on the tradeoff

Same STAR shape as Day 4. What makes a negotiation story land is showing you framed the ask around a tradeoff, not a demand.

- **Situation** — what was being proposed (a deadline, a scope, a headcount) that you believed was wrong.
- **Task** — what you were negotiating for and why it mattered.
- **Action** — how you made the case: what data or tradeoff you presented, who you talked to, what you offered in exchange.
- **Result** — what you actually got (rarely 100% of the ask), and why the compromise worked.

The tell of a weak negotiation story is "I asked for more time and got it" with no mechanism. A strong one names a specific tradeoff: "I offered to cut X if we kept Y."

## Worked example

> **Situation**: "Our PM wanted a new reporting feature shipped in two weeks to hit a customer commitment, but the honest estimate from the team was closer to four, given the data model changes required. **Task**: I was the engineer who'd scoped it, so it fell to me to either commit to two weeks or make the case for something else. **Action**: Instead of just saying 'no, four weeks,' I broke the feature into a version that shipped in two weeks with a manually-refreshed report instead of live data, and a follow-up phase for the real-time version. I brought that split to the PM with the actual data-model risk laid out, framed as 'here's what two weeks buys you and what it doesn't.' **Result**: The PM took the phased version to the customer, who accepted a manual-refresh MVP as long as real-time landed within the month. We hit both dates, the customer stayed, and phased scoping became the default way we handled tight deadlines on that team afterward."

Notice the negotiation wasn't "more time" — it was a scope split that gave the other side a real choice.

## Your template

```
Situation: [what was being proposed — deadline, scope, resources — that felt wrong]
Task: [what you were negotiating for]
Action: [the tradeoff or data you presented] + [who you brought it to] + [what you offered in exchange]
Result: [what you actually got] + [why the compromise worked]
```

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Frame the ask as a tradeoff, not a demand | Say "I pushed back and they agreed" with no mechanism |
| Show what you offered in exchange | Make it sound like a one-sided win |
| Quantify the outcome | Leave the result vague |
