## Why this review matters

By Day 21 you've built ten-plus STAR stories, situational answers, and company-specific research. This is the last full review before mock interviews start around Day 30, so the goal isn't just to check completeness — it's to find the actual gaps that would embarrass you in a real interview, while there's still time to close them.

## The technique: stress-test, don't just inventory

Go through each story category — resume, project deep-dives, conflict, production failure, leadership, negotiation, innovation, obstacle, success, new skill — and ask two questions: could I answer this cold, with no notes, in under three minutes? And does this story have a number in it? Anything that fails either question isn't done.

Then check company-specific prep against your actual target list from Day 15 — research that was accurate three weeks ago may be stale if a company shipped something new or changed direction.

## Doing the review

List every remaining gap honestly rather than assuming Week 4's stories will cover it — Week 4 covers new categories (communication, mentorship, difficult coworker, career goals, asking for help, feedback, ownership), not a second pass on Week 1-3 material. If something from Weeks 1-3 is still shaky, schedule time to fix it before Day 30, because mock interviews are where a shaky story gets exposed in front of another person instead of privately.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Test every story cold, without notes | Assume having written it once is enough |
| Refresh company research that's gone stale | Reuse Day 15 research unchanged three weeks later |
| Write down specific gaps, not "I should review more" | Leave weaknesses vague and unscheduled |
