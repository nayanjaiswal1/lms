## Why interviewers ask this

"Walk me through your resume" or "tell me about yourself" is the first data point in every interview. Nobody wants your life story here; they want proof you can communicate clearly, prioritize what matters, and connect your background to the role you're interviewing for. The first impression happens before a single technical question is asked.

## The framework: Present → Past → Future

A strong walkthrough is three tight paragraphs, in this order:

1. **Present**: who you are right now. Current role, company (one line of context if it's not well known), and the stack or domain you work in.
2. **Past**: the one or two achievements that best set up the rest of the interview. Lead with impact, not a task list, and attach a metric wherever you can.
3. **Future**: why you're looking to move, and what you want next. Point this directly at the role you're interviewing for, not a generic "growth opportunity" line.

Total spoken length: **90-120 seconds**. If you're still talking at 3 minutes, you've included too much and the interviewer will pull the thread on anything they want more of.

Weak answers list every job since college in chronological order with no throughline. Strong answers pick the 20% of your history that's relevant to this interview and compress everything else into a half-sentence.

## Worked example

> "I'm currently a backend engineer at a logistics startup, working mostly in Go and Postgres building the systems that route delivery orders to drivers. Before that, I spent three years at a mid-size fintech company, where I led the rebuild of our payments reconciliation pipeline. It cut manual reconciliation time from two days a month to under an hour, and it's still the system of record there today. I'm looking to move now because I've hit the ceiling of what a 15-person engineering team can throw at me. I want to work on problems at higher scale, with more experienced engineers around me, which is what drew me to this role."

One metric, one system named, one honest reason for moving. That's the whole recipe.

## Your template

```
Present: I'm currently a [role] at [company], where I [what you do day to day, name the stack/domain].

Past: Before that / also at [company], I [project or achievement], which [what changed because of it, with a number].

Future: I'm looking to move now because [specific, honest reason], and I'm drawn to [this company/role] because [specific connection].
```

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with impact and numbers | List job titles with no context |
| Keep it under 2 minutes | Narrate your entire career history |
| End on a reason tied to this role | Trail off with "and that's about it" |
| Practice out loud, not only in your head | Read it verbatim from a script |
| Flag 3 deep-dive-worthy projects | Mention ten projects at equal weight |
