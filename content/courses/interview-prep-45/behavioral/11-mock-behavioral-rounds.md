## What a mock behavioral round looks like

A real behavioral round has a shape, and rehearsing that shape matters as much as rehearsing any single story. Run this script alone, out loud, with a timer, or with a partner reading the prompts:

- **"Tell me about yourself" (2 minutes).** Budget it: 30 seconds on your current role, 45 seconds on relevant background, 30 seconds on why you're looking now, 15 seconds transitioning into what excites you. This is a 90-second pitch that sets up the rest of the interview, not your life story.
- **"Why do you want to join?" (3 minutes).** Name something specific about the company you actually researched, connect it to what you want next, and end on a concrete thing you'd want to work on there.
- **A project deep dive (10 minutes), told in STAR.** Situation, 1 minute. Task, 1 minute. Action, 5-6 minutes, where most of the time goes; be ready to go two levels deep on any technical claim. Result, 2 minutes: a quantified outcome plus what you'd do differently now.

Vague answers, like "we improved performance a lot," are the single most common behavioral failure. The fix is rehearsing with real numbers pulled from your own projects before the interview, not during it.

> **Remember:** a mock round only works if you run the whole shape, not just your favorite story in isolation.

## Running the story-based round yourself

Pull 4-5 questions from your story bank, out of order, plus at least one you don't have a prepped story for. Use a different real story for each prompt: reusing the same anecdote for every question is a red flag interviewers notice fast.

Grade yourself on:

- Each answer under 2 minutes, clearly STAR-shaped.
- Filler words near zero.
- Specificity: a real metric, decision, or outcome, not a generic one ("I cut p99 latency from 800ms to 140ms," not "I improved performance").
- Handling a "why" or "what would you do differently" follow-up without just repeating the original answer.

Across common categories like conflict resolution, a technical challenge, leadership, and failure, aim for roughly 20% Situation and Task combined, 60% Action, 20% Result. The Action section is where most candidates under-invest: "I fixed it" is not an action, "I profiled the service, found the lock contention was in the connection pool, and split it into per-shard pools" is. If you can't fill 3 minutes of concrete action detail on a story, it's the wrong story.

> **Remember:** if a follow-up question makes you repeat your first answer instead of adding detail, the story wasn't rehearsed deep enough.

## Running the situational round yourself

Situational questions are hypotheticals, not past-experience stories: "your teammate keeps merging code without tests, what do you do?" or "you disagree with your manager's technical decision, walk me through it." These test judgment, not memory.

Grade yourself on:

- Stating the principle first ("I'd raise it directly and early"), then the specific steps.
- Showing both directness and collaboration: neither pure compliance nor pure confrontation.
- Tying the hypothetical back to an actual past experience, if you have one, even briefly.

> **Remember:** a situational answer states the principle first, then the steps. Jumping straight to steps with no stated principle reads as improvised.

## The compression test: 10 words per STAR beat

A useful final check for any story in your bank: recite its four STAR beats, Situation, Task, Action, Result, in roughly 10 words each, from memory. If you can't compress a story that tightly without losing the measurable outcome, it's still too padded with backstory to survive a real 2-minute time cap.

## Common mistakes

- Reusing the same anecdote across multiple prompts in the same mock round.
- Rushing or skipping the "why do you want to join" question because it feels like small talk.
- Answering a situational question with only steps and no stated principle.
- Treating a mock round as done after one pass, instead of grading yourself and running it again.

> **Remember:** a story you can't compress into 10 words per beat isn't ready for a 2-minute time cap.
