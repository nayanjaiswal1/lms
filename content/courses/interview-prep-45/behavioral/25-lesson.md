## Why interviewers ask this

"Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" isn't asking you to predict the future. It's checking two things: will you stay long enough to be worth hiring, and does your trajectory actually make sense with this role. An answer with nothing to do with the job you're interviewing for signals you're not actually aligned with it.

## The framework: near-term concrete, long-term directional

**1-year goal**: specific and achievable, a skill deepened, a scope of ownership, a type of project. This should sound like something the role you're interviewing for would directly produce.

**5-year goal**: directional, not a job-title ladder. "I want to be a staff engineer" is weaker than "I want to be the person a team turns to for system design decisions in [domain]," because the second is about capability, not a title, and holds up regardless of how the company levels people.

**The connective thread**: explicitly tie both back to why this role is a reasonable step toward them, not a random data point in your career.

Avoid two failure modes: goals so vague they could apply to anyone ("I want to keep growing and learning"), and goals that don't need this specific job at all (management-track goals in an IC interview, founder goals in an interview at a large company).

## Worked example

> "In the next year, I want to go deeper on distributed systems specifically. Right now I've worked adjacent to sharding and replication but haven't owned that kind of design decision end-to-end, which is exactly the kind of work this backend platform team does. In five years, I want to be the engineer people bring hard data-consistency problems to before they've fully scoped them. Not necessarily a management track; I want to stay hands-on, but with enough depth that I'm shaping architecture decisions, not just implementing them. This role is a direct step toward that because the platform team owns exactly the kind of infrastructure decisions I want more reps on."

Notice the 5-year goal is a capability, not a title, and both years connect explicitly back to this specific role.

## Your template

```
1-year: I want to [specific skill/scope], which is exactly what [this role] would give me through [specific project/team responsibility].

5-year: I want to be [capability-based description, not just a title], [what that would look like day to day].

Connection: This role is a step toward that because [specific, honest link].
```

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Make the 1-year goal concrete and role-relevant | Say "I want to keep growing and learning" |
| Frame the 5-year goal around capability, not title | Recite a generic career ladder |
| Tie both goals explicitly to this role | Describe a trajectory this job wouldn't advance |
